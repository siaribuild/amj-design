One verification, three doors, and never a percentage.

A tradie proves their business once. If the ABN, the business name and the email domain all check out against the ABR, trade pricing is theirs in that same interaction — while they configure, not after they quote. Anything else goes to a person. Nothing is ever auto-rejected, and the customer is never told which check needed a human.

Trade pricing is described only as better prices. No percentage appears on any customer surface, and no pair of figures from which one could be derived by subtraction. Phase 1's AC-41 silence about trade ends here by design — these are the only surfaces where trade pricing is mentioned (AC-P2-48). A tester finding trade copy here is recording conformance, not a regression.

Two outcomes, never three

Active, or we're checking. There is no machine rejection: an ABR outage, a gmail address and a name that didn't quite match all land in the same under-review screen, worded identically.

No timeframe, ever

Nothing on screen or in any email says how long a review takes — no "one business day", no "usually", no date. "We'll be in touch" is the whole promise (owner ruling Q2).

A refusal costs nothing

A rejected application leaves a fully working private account — quoting, submitting, tracking, referrals. The screen says exactly that, and offers to apply again.

Verified and pending at once

A verified account applying with a new ABN keeps its trade pricing while the new one is checked. Every screen renders both facts; no single status word is used anywhere.

Surface 1 · Door (a) — marketing page, universal flow

/trade-account hosts the same signup everyone else gets

There is one registration flow in this product — email, 6-digit code, your details — and Phase 2 does not add a second one. What it adds is an optional ABN + business-name group inside the details, the same group that appears at the submit gate and on the account card. The fork is what happens after: an ABN was entered, so verification runs and one of the two outcome panels appears; no ABN, and this is an ordinary private account exactly as Phase 1 shipped it.

The trade page's job is to explain and to carry trade intent — hero, benefits, the trade copy — not to own a flow. Its only difference from every other entry point is that the optional group arrives already revealed, because someone who navigated here came specifically to hand over an ABN.

One flow, one optional group — and what actually differs here

This is the Phase-1 signup, in the Phase-1 order, with the Phase-1 copy: email → code → your details. The optional business group is the same group as the submit gate's (surface 4) and the account card's (surface 3) — same labels, same helpers, same validation, same rule that a business name becomes required only once an ABN is present.

Exactly one thing is entry-point-specific: on /trade-account the group renders already revealed, because a visitor who navigated to the trade page came to hand over an ABN and should be able to type it where they expect to. Everywhere else it stays collapsed until it is relevant. Nothing else forks — not the order, not the components, not the copy.

The group sits after the email field and before the human check because no unauthenticated endpoint may accept an ABN: the values are held in browser state and posted only once the session exists (AC-P2-3). The caption is what stops that delay reading as "it ignored what I typed".

This replaces the first draft's ordering, on the owner's correction

The first draft composed a bespoke card — "Your business" first, then "Your account" — arguing that the trade question should come first because it is why the visitor is on the page. The owner rejected the premise: "I don't understand why are we creating a new registration flow for business users, rather than simply adding a field within one established in P1?" That is the stronger argument. A second registration form is a second thing to build, test, and keep in step forever, and the conversion nicety it bought does not pay for that. One flow, one optional group, one fork — after the fact.

Never an ABN-guessing oracle

The checksum is the only thing checked in the browser, and it is the same function the Worker uses (src/data/abn.ts). Everything past it — whether the ABN exists, whether it's active, whether the name matches — happens server-side behind a session and is never reported back field-by-field. That is a deliberate security property, not a copy decision.

A malformed ABN blocks the step it is on, exactly as it blocks Submit at the gate: silently discarding what someone typed is worse, and Phase 1 forbids an unexplained disabled button. Clearing the field always releases it.

Work tone, not warning tone

The panel uses the console-blue "in progress" tone the product already spends on things underway, with a clock — never amber, never a triangle. Nothing has gone wrong and the copy never implies it might: no "unfortunately", no "we couldn't", no "if approved".

Two timeframes must never touch

Phase 1's confirmation promises a quote-review response in 1–2 business days. That promise is about the quote and only the quote. In the first draft of this mock the ABN block sat two lines under it, and a fast reader would have carried the 1–2 days onto the verification — a turnaround promise by adjacency, which the owner's ruling forbids as absolutely as one in words. The block now sits after the actions, behind a rule, and says out loud that the response time above is not its own.

The one sentence that must not creep

"Your quote isn't waiting on it" is the whole truth and the whole promise. It must never become "and we'll update your quote if it's approved" — ops prices a reviewed quote at the account's rate when they review it, and nothing recalculates behind the customer's back (P2-D4). A repricing promise here would be a promise the system does not keep.

Surface 5 · Ops console

A queue that only ever holds judgment calls

Clean passes never appear here — they were approved by the machine. What lands in this queue is a gmail sole trader, a name that didn't quite match, an ABN the register says is cancelled, an outage, or a second account claiming an ABN someone else already holds. Each arrives with the evidence already gathered, so a decision is a read and a click. Any staff member with an assigned role can clear it (owner ruling Q4) — not admins only.

No greeting line, on purpose

Accounts can exist with no name on file, and the template engine renders a missing variable as an empty string — so Hi {name}, becomes Hi , for exactly the customers least likely to forgive it. None of the four opens with a greeting; the only variable used in a body is {business}, and only in the one email where a business name is guaranteed to exist.

Appendix

What must not appear, and what changed since Phase 1

Absence assertions — things a test proves, not things a reviewer remembers

No percentage, anywhere customer-facing

Not on the trade page, the account card, the gate, the confirmation, or any of the four emails — and no pair of figures from which one could be derived by subtraction. The ops console is the single exception and is not a customer surface.

No timeframe

No "1 business day", no "usually", no date, no progress bar implying elapsed time. Not in UI, not in email.

No named criterion

No screen or email tells the customer which of the checks needed a human. The two customer-facing outcomes are byte-identical across every queue reason.

No builder/tradie control at the submit gate

The gate asks for ABN and business name only. The label is asked on the trade page and the account page, and reads "Not stated" for ops.

No referral-code field

Nothing this phase adds carries one, and no endpoint it adds accepts one.

No repricing promise

Nothing says a submitted quote will be updated. Ops prices a reviewed quote at the account's current rate; nothing recalculates on its own.

Phase 1's AC-41 silence ends here, by design

Phase 1 asserted that no Phase-1 surface mentions trade pricing at all. Phase 2 deliberately adds that copy to exactly four surfaces: /trade-account, the account page's trade card, the submit gate's optional-ABN helper text, and the outcome emails. Everywhere else — home page, nav, quote builder, review screen pricing panels — stays silent; the shop window is Phase 3's job. The Phase-1 Playwright assertions covering these four are updated openly, citing the Phase-2 spec. A tester finding trade copy on these four surfaces is recording conformance, not a regression.

Where the words live

Every string in these frames is final copy and is repeated, with its focus order, ARIA behaviour, loading and disabled states, in the interaction spec appended to the architect's design as §18 of docs/specs/user-registration-phase-2-design.md. The mock is the picture; that section is the contract.
