Phase 1 · docs/specs/user-registration-phase-1.md

Anonymity ends at one button, and only at that one.

Browsing, configuring, live prices, autosave and uploads are unchanged — nobody is asked for anything. Submit for technical review is the single point where an account becomes required, because quote review is the most expensive human time in the business.

The friction is deliberate: an extra minute won't turn away a serious buyer, but it will turn away someone wandering around. It must never feel broken or hostile — so every screen here announces the cost before charging it, and keeps the quote itself visible the whole way through.

One screen, four stages

The gate lives inside the existing review screen. The quote panel above it never disappears — the customer is always looking at what they are about to submit.

Never auto-submit

Verifying a code signs you in. It does not submit your quote. Submission stays the customer's deliberate act (A-P1-4).

Nothing about trade

No trade pricing, trade account, discount or application appears anywhere on these screens (AC-41). No referral-code field exists on any form (AC-32).

Invisible when satisfied

A returning customer with complete details sees no gate at all — one press of Submit, exactly as today.

Why the line is there

AC-11 requires the sign-in step to appear when Submit is pressed — so Submit stays the one primary action and the screen never opens with an email field in the customer's face. The cost is announced first; the click that imposes it is theirs.

Nothing on this screen says "no account needed", and nothing says "trade". The old contact panel (name/email/phone) is gone from the anonymous view entirely — identity now comes from the session.

Legible, not alarming

Three things carry the moment: a plain-language notice that states what happened and why, an item count in the panel label, and a Just added chip on the lines that moved. Nothing is coloured as a warning — a merge is a convenience, not a fault.

The notice and the chips persist until the customer submits or leaves the screen. They are not dismissible: their whole job is to be present at the moment of the decision.

Must not happen

Submit must be unreachable — not merely disabled-looking — while the re-resolve is in flight, and the delivery estimate must be re-fetched against the surviving project id (E6). A stale estimate shown against a different project is the failure this stage exists to prevent.

Surface 6

The GST flip — same screen, no reload

Why the extra line

Numbers dropping by ~9% at the exact moment someone signs in reads as a bug — or worse, as a bait. One quiet caption explains it and points at the setting. It appears only on the render immediately following a sign-in on this screen, never on a normal visit.

No discount percentage, and no pair of figures a discount could be derived from by subtraction, appears anywhere (AC-36).

Where the account gets edited

The gate is the account's editing surface as well as its collection surface (AC-19): an edited value is written to the account on submit and pre-fills every future quote. Delivery is the exception — it belongs to the project, and overwriting it never touches the account address (AC-42).
