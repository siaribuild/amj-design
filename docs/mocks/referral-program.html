Read this first

Every screen the referral program adds, in the site's own design language.

This is what the program will look like. The colours, type sizes, spacing, buttons, cards and status chips are the real ones taken from the live site — the only visual difference from production is the typeface, because a mock is not allowed to fetch anything from the internet and the site's two fonts live on Google Fonts.

Revision 2. Joining is now an explicit step — read the offer, join and accept the conditions, then add bank details. The landing hero is cut to one sentence. The discount card has moved into the Referrals section, so that section now has to work for someone who was referred and hasn't joined. The payout threshold is gone entirely, the program is On/Off rather than three states, and the ops screens are stripped to the minimum that does the job.

Every word here is real copy. If you approve this, this is what a tradie reads. Numbers shown are the launch defaults — 2.5%, 1%, $2,000, 12 months, 14 days — and every one of them is a slot filled from the ops console, so changing any of them later needs no code and no new words.

Screens are shown at real widths: desktop 1180px and phone 375px. Scroll a frame to see the whole screen where it is taller than the box.

Never a total discount

The referred tradie sees 2.5%, never 7.5%. Every registered account already gets a standing 5% that is deliberately invisible, and a total gives it away by subtraction. No screen here prints a combined figure.

Referring is never tied to buying

Nothing says "order first, then you can earn". That coupling is a strict-liability offence (ACL s 49). "Any account can join" and "we need your bank details" now live on different steps, so they can't read as one condition.

No invite form, anywhere

There is no screen on which you type a mate's name, phone or email. You share your own code, your own way. That boundary is what keeps the whole business outside a Privacy Act trigger.

The conditions sit next to the offer

Minimum order, what the 1% is on, paid-on-payment, the 12-month lapse, the ABN and the 14 days appear beside the headline — not in a footer. Fine print cannot rescue a headline claim.

What changed, and why

The hero is a headline and one sentence. It was a headline, a subline, a bordered rule box, two numbered facts and two buttons — six things competing to be read first, which is why it had to be concentrated on rather than glanced at. The program is one sentence; the hero is now that sentence.

Banking has left this page entirely. The hero says what you get and offers one button. The "we need your ABN and bank details" fact now lives on step 2 of joining, which means the two facts the law requires to stay separate are no longer even on the same screen — a stronger separation than typography was giving them.

One grid on the page, and it's the FAQ. Two card grids in sequence read as monotony whatever is in them — and six conditions at equal weight in a bordered panel is the same as no hierarchy, which is why it read as a table. The fix wasn't a third arrangement of six items. It was asking which conditions change whether a tradie bothers.

Three of the six were never conditions. The 14 days was already in the offer sentence. The "1% of the goods, excluding GST and delivery" is the basis of the headline claim, so it belongs inside step 3 rather than in a list under it. The 12 months is a property of the mate's discount, so it sits in step 2. All three moved into the offer, where they read as part of the deal instead of caveats on it.

Two moved off the page — no self-referral and the cancelled-order detail — into the terms and the FAQ article. Neither changes whether someone shares a code.

Three remain, because each one decides it. The minimum order (if your mates buy small, this is worth nothing to you), first-order-only (it bounds the upside — one payment per mate, not a cut of everything after), and the ABN (a precondition on you). They're three hairline-separated rows in body-weight type, one band under the offer. Compact is not fine print: the prose is the same size as the offer's own body copy, and the one caption-sized line on the band carries no conditions at all — just a tax note and two links.

The join band is black. It's the conversion moment on a page of otherwise light content, and the only band whose job is to be acted on — so it gets the only other dark treatment, and the sage button becomes the strongest thing on the page. It works because it sits mid-page: the FAQ and the closing banner stand between it and the black footer. Put this band last and it would dissolve into the footer, which is why it stays fourth of six.

Nothing on it is a card. A card's fill comes from its section's ground, and the site defines that only for the two light grounds — so a card on black would come out light. The site's own dark sections put content straight on the ground, and this does the same.

Signed in, the same band is light. By then it holds a form, and a bank-details form on black is wrong. The dark treatment is for prompting, not for working.

Six bands, and the texture alternates: dark hero → card track → text rows → black band → card track → sage block. Grounds run night → bone → paper → night → paper → bone, following the sequence ContactPage and ProductsPage already use. No two grids touch, and the two dark bands have two light ones between them.

Every bold number is a slot. Raising the discount to 5% changes six figures on this page and not one word.

Why the join flow earns its place

It moves the bank-account ask behind a decision. Nobody is shown a banking field until they've said they want in. That was the single biggest problem with the previous design, and it is solved structurally rather than by better wording.

It creates an acceptance at a specific instant. Before this there was no moment anywhere in the product at which anyone agreed to anything — the terms were published prose. Now there is a timestamp, a terms version and a person, and because membership commits in the same act, there is no window in which someone has accepted terms but isn't in the program.

The tick-box here is not the ops-style acknowledgement. The person clicking is the person entering the agreement, and the click is part of the operative act. That is the opposite of a staff member ticking "I understand" about an obligation they cannot affect — and there are none of those anywhere in this design.

"Continue", not "Join". The button that starts part 2 must not claim something happened. Joining happens once, on the last button.

State C The same flow, part 2 nothing committed yet

Not a membership state — the second half of one uncommitted flow. Pressing the button accepts the conditions, stores the payout details, creates the membership and issues the code in one act. Close the tab here and none of it happened; the join CTA is simply there again next time. The form is the same component the account area uses, so the words can never drift apart.

One commit, and no state to be stuck in

Abandoning here is not a state. No half-member, no dormant record, no screen explaining a limbo, no email chasing a partial joiner. They come back and see the join CTA again. The only thing that survives is what they typed, if they navigate away within the session — ordinary form behaviour, not a membership fact.

The button says "Join the program", and that is literally true of it. Accepting the conditions, storing the details, creating the membership and issuing the code are one act at one instant. Nothing anywhere has to reason about a user who accepted terms but isn't a member.

The headline is about money, not paperwork. "Where do we send the money?" presumes the money is coming. "Complete your profile" would read as onboarding admin.

What they get is stated before what we want. The right-hand panel shows the deal and a greyed-out example code — a preview of the thing on the other side of the form.

"Back" returns to the conditions without losing anything typed here.

Still worth saying out loud

The bank-details ask is smaller than it was — it now arrives after a decision instead of before one, and it is never the first thing anyone reads about the program. It is still an ask, and some people will stop at it. Because nothing is recorded until the final button, we cannot tell those people apart from people who never got that far; if you want that number later it has to be front-end analytics, not a database state.

Two states, not three — and switching off never takes anything back

"Paused" and "ended" said different things to a reader but did exactly the same thing to the system: stop recording, hide the placements, honour everything already promised. One flag, two states. Turning it back on attributes nothing retroactively — referrals that lapsed while it was off stay lapsed.

The copy commits to honouring in the same breath as the ending, rather than reserving a right to change its mind. Stopping something already promised needs a deliberate per-referral void in ops; it can never be a side effect of this switch.

Treating the referred tradie properly

Their thing comes first. They arrived to look at their discount. The join invitation sits below it, in a muted card with an outline button rather than a sage one — present, not pushy, and visibly the second subject on the page.

The invitation argues from what just happened to them. "You got 2.5% off because someone passed you a code. You can do the same." They are the single best-qualified audience for this, because they have experienced the benefit from the other side — so the copy says exactly that and then stops.

Neither state shows a form. Joining is a flow; this is a door. Nobody sees a banking field until they have chosen to go through it.

What is deliberately not here: no empty table with five headers, no "0 referrals · $0.00 earned" strip, no greyed-out share buttons. Until there is a code there is nothing to report.

Three decisions on this screen

Two blocks, always in the same order. "Your discount" then "Refer a mate", with an h2 on each. Someone who has both never has to work out which half they're reading, and someone who has one simply doesn't see the other heading.

Money first inside the referrer block. Pending, Confirmed, Paid — the dashboard's own summary strip. Confirmed is the only cell with a tone, because it is the only one with a date attached, and it always names the day the payment is due.

Never a dollar figure against a person. The list carries a business name (or a masked email) and a status word. What their order was worth is theirs.

The status list, cut from six to five

The old set was Signed up · Quoting · Ordered · Paid in full · Not eligible · Expired. "Quoting" is gone: it tells a referrer their mate is shopping, which is neither theirs to know nor anything they can act on, and it never changes when they get paid. What is left answers the only two questions the screen exists for — is this happening, and when do I get paid.

Signed up (they have an account) → Ordered (your share is pending) → Paid (your share is confirmed, with a date). Plus two endings: Expired and Not eligible, each with one line saying why.

A referral voided by staff shows as Not eligible with a generic line. The ops reason is an internal record and is not shown to the customer.

A percentage plus an earned amount is invertible: 1% and $45.00 tells a referrer their mate spent $4,500. The rules this card is built on

One percentage, and no other. 2.5%, never a combined figure, never a reference to any other discount. This is the single thing most likely to get "improved" into a total later — a total gives away the standing discount by subtraction just as effectively as printing it.

No redemption language anywhere. Not apply, redeem, claim, use at checkout, voucher, credit or balance. There is no redemption moment in this system — the discount is applied when the price is computed — and copy that implies otherwise creates support calls and an expectation of a further reduction on top of the price already shown.

The expiry is in the headline, not the footnote. Under 30 days it becomes the headline itself, the spine turns amber, and the CTA changes to finishing the quote. That is also when the reminder email goes out, so the screen and the inbox agree.

Nothing here moves with the ex/inc GST toggle, because a percentage is unit-free. That is arithmetic, not an exemption — the prices it acted on keep honouring the preference everywhere else.

One thing this card can't fix

A referred tradie has nothing to check "2.5% off" against. The site publishes no list price, so the figure is unverifiable by design — which is exactly what makes showing the increment alone safe, and also what means its credibility rests entirely on tone. A tradie who priced a job anonymously before signing up will see the price move by more than 2.5%, because the standing account discount arrives at the same moment. That gap exists today for every registering customer; the referral is what gives them a number to check it against.

Why it sits above the figures

The prompt and the discount row share one slot. Resting, it asks; applied, it reports. The thing that appears is the thing the field produced, in the place the field was — which is the clearest way to say the change has already happened.

Above the total, never below it. A field under a total is a checkout coupon box, and this site has no redemption step. Above the figures it reads as a statement about what follows.

"Prices below have come down" on the first render after applying, then "Already in the prices above" on every render after. Same row, one clause of difference, and the customer sees the movement they just caused.

One refusal message for three causes — unknown code, a code whose owner has left the program, and a code belonging to the same business. Naming which would tell whoever typed it something about a third party's account.

It disappears completely once the first order exists or the account already has a referral. No greyed field, no "already used" note — nothing.

Where it does NOT appear, and why that matters

Not on an issued quote. An issued quote is a price offer the customer can accept; applying a code there would re-price it under them, which AC-54 forbids. The money panel is the same component at all three stages, so the prompt is gated to the pre-issue ones — the draft in the builder and the submitted project — and the issued quote and the order never render it.

This is pricing, not a placement. Spec §8.2 says never interrupt a priced flow with a marketing offer, and at a glance this looks like one. It is the same category as the discount row it replaces: a statement about the price, on the price surface. It carries no pitch, no commission rate and no link to /refer — just the field and what it does.

A referrer who wants to check their percentage has to leave the account area. That is the price of taking the rate off the earnings screen, and it is one click via How the program works.

What changes if you change a number

Nothing here is written around 2.5% or 1%. Raise the discount to 5%, drop the minimum to $1,000, shorten the window to six months — every figure on every screen re-renders and not one sentence needs rewriting. That is why the numbers are always followed by a noun ("2.5% off their first order", "$2,000 ex GST", "12 months") rather than fused into the grammar.
